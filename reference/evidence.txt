Plans and Pricing ? Voice Transcript
Initial idea: Free, about $20, and about $40 with unlimited use. Unlimited was later judged
unsustainable due to computation/server load and product value. Final described structure:
Free, Pro about $5/month, Premium about $20/month. Free demonstrated the app with Pro access
initially 30 days, later about 15 days by business/client decision. Annual discounts
averaged 17%. Pro included selected Pro tools/custom features. Pro and Premium offered PDF
watchlist export and configurable share links; Premium included a portfolio dashboard. One
lead registered and tried it; no paid subscriptions because the application remained
incomplete. Lesson: price according to value, costs, effort, and user needs; avoid
over/undervaluing.

4. Typing versus Talking ? Personalized Copy
Typing a piece of text took about 5 minutes 20 seconds; voice input took about 1 minute 30
seconds. The author is dictating this project material with Wispr Flow and then using AI to
organize it for Emergent. This makes it possible to produce more material quickly; the
estimated 80?90% time saving is personal experience, not a general benchmark.
Voice input helps with long documents, prompts, and substantial writing. It reduces reliance
on keyboard access and typing speed. Typing still gives more precise control. Voice is
useful when volume and speed matter; choice depends on task and environment.
After two to three days, the author noticed automatic corrections can change intended
wording. Reviewing a long transcript may take time. Voice input also depends on comfort with
pronunciation, intonation, sentence formation, and punctuation; background noise reduces
accuracy. Sensitive information such as passwords, bank details, or confidential messages
may be unsuitable for dictation. Regional-language support can reduce the English-language
barrier for some users. These are personal observations, not universal claims.
